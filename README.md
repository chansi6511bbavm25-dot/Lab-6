This laboratory focuses on security, privacy, data protection, and responsible use of Generative AI in the aviation industry. The main objective is to identify sensitive information, understand privacy risks, and learn how to create safe AI prompts without exposing personal or confidential data.

The lab begins by identifying different types of sensitive aviation information, including passenger names, passport numbers, Aadhaar numbers, mobile numbers, personal emails, booking references, flight details, medical information, and payment-card information. Each type of information is analysed according to its risk and an appropriate action is suggested, such as removing the information completely or replacing it with a safe placeholder.

The lab introduces the use of placeholders such as [PASSENGER NAME], [BOOKING REFERENCE], [FLIGHT NUMBER], and [VERIFY AIRLINE POLICY]. Placeholders allow the structure of an AI task to remain intact while reducing the exposure of real personal or confidential information.

A privacy-safe baggage-delay prompt is created using these principles. It avoids passport, Aadhaar, mobile, email, medical, and payment information and prevents unsupported promises about delivery timelines or compensation. The lab also compares unsafe and safe prompts to demonstrate how removing sensitive information and adding verification instructions improves privacy and security.

The lab further classifies information into public, internal, confidential, and restricted/highly sensitive categories. It covers practical PII challenges involving flight cancellations, special assistance, employee reports, and payment complaints. Safe rewritten prompts are developed for each situation.

Finally, the security audit checklist and data-minimisation activity reinforce that only necessary information should be shared with AI tools. Important information such as baggage status, compensation, and operational policies should be verified through authorised systems. Overall, LAB 6 develops responsible AI usage, privacy awareness, prompt-safety, data-minimisation, and human-review skills required for professional aviation applications.
